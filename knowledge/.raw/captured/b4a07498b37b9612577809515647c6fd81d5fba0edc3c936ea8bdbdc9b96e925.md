|     | #### More Definitions |
| Here are a few more definitions required for a complete understanding of the next chapter.<br><br>*   long - a position with a net positive quantity. A long position is not necessarily a bullish position. A long PUT profits from a decline in the underlying stock price, and is therefore bearish.<br>*   short - a position with a net negative quantity. In the stock and option markets, it is possible to sell a security you do not already own. A short sale of a stock is effectively borrowing a stock with the obligation to buy it back later, hopefully at a lower price. In the options market, short selling an option is writing a contract to deliver the underlying security, if the option buyer exercises the option.<br>*   Option Chain - the list of all the options associated with the same underlying security.<br>*   American Style - an option that can be exercised at any time<br>*   European Style - an option that can only be exercised at expiration<br>*   Exercise - to invoke the right associated with the option contract, i.e. to take a position in the underlying stock by exercising the right of the option contract<br>*   Early exercise - invoking the right of the option contract before expiration<br>*   Assignment - being forced to deliver the underlying stock to fulfill the obligation incurred when writing an option contract<br>*   Theoretical price - The computed price of an option, independent of the actual market price . The theoretical price is often computed and compared to market prices in an effort to find disparities in market prices.<br>*   Historical Volatility - the measure of the likelihood of a stock price to change. This is typically expressed as the annualized standard deviation of price changes in the underlying stock for a period of 3,6 or 12 months.<br>*   Implied volatility - the volatility which is computed using the current market price, and other known, fixed model inputs. For example, if the theoretical price can be computed using a formula where the volatility is known:<br>    <br>    > price = f(historical\_volatility, strike, term, rate, yield)<br>    <br>    then the volatility can be computed using a similar formula if the price is known<br>    <br>    > implied\_volatility = f(price, strike, term, rate, yield)<br>    <br><br>The factors effecting option prices are discussed in [Chapter 3](https://www.optionistics.com/s/chapter_3). |
